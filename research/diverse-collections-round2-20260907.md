# Diverse collection additions, round two — 7 September 2026

28 works approved for publication after local review: 8 African, 17 from the Americas, 2 Oceanian, and 1 Asian. The public collection grows from 535 to 563 works. The sources represent eight collections: the African Museum in Olkusz, Barcelona's Museu Etnològic i de Cultures del Món, Williams College Museum of Art, the Bonn Collection of the Americas, Chile's Museo Nacional de Historia Natural, the Hunt Museum, Museo Virtual Nariño, and Yale Peabody Museum.

The batch includes two works from the 2019 Carnaval de Negros y Blancos in Pasto, Colombia, and two examples of Barniz de Pasto decoration. Museo Virtual Nariño is identified as a digital collection; no unstated current physical location is asserted. Dates, makers, media and accessions are left unspecified where the source does not supply them. The Giravolt vessel from the Nazca Valley retains its publisher's 800–200 BCE dating; geography is not treated as proof of Nazca cultural attribution. The Kabyle pitcher has an explicit Africa wing assignment and is not classified as sub-Saharan.

Per-object source records, current publisher licenses, archive download URLs, source and preview SHA-256 hashes, metadata qualifications and orientation notes are in `diverse-collections-round2-20260907.json`. Every selected archive is CC0 or CC BY 4.0. Current Sketchfab publisher metadata was checked for 27 objects; the Hunt Museum's HCM 305 fragment comes directly from its museum-authored Zenodo archive. Source downloads were checked against archive size and MD5 and parsed as GLB before conversion. Existing slugs, source model identifiers, archive records and available accessions were checked for duplicates.

All 28 were compared visually with publisher reference images in Atrium's renderer. Rotation alternatives were rendered where the default view was ambiguous. The Royal Mask, Ile Ori, jaguar whistle, Mágicas y Peligrosas and Krishna were turned to reveal their fronts. The stone fish vessel was rotated so its basin faces upward. The Mbuya mask's separately named scanning board was removed and its source transforms normalized, with the whole mask and raffia retained. Legacy specular/glossiness materials were converted for the Royal Mask, Mbuya mask and Cofre. Node transforms were baked for three models to improve framing. No surface colors were invented.

Bernini's *Ecstasy of Saint Teresa* was investigated but is not included. Searches found paid digital recreations, a non-downloadable Cornaro Chapel model, an independently sculpted bust, and a different relief. None provides a usable open scan of the complete Bernini group. Evidence and exclusion reasons are recorded in the JSON manifest.
