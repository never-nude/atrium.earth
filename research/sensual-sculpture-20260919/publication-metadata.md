## Aphrodite

**Unknown Greek artist, 400–200 BCE.** Terracotta. Height: 43.5 cm (17 1/8 in.). Cleveland Museum of Art, accession 1927.489.

[Object record](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/1927.489) · [3D model](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/1927489-aphrodite-50080844b44d4e1eb244ba33fc00a452) · [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/)

- **Object identity:** Museum accession, model title, material and image all agree.
- **Artwork rights:** Public domain; museum marks this work available for copying, modification and distribution without permission.
- **Scan attribution:** Aphrodite, 400–200 BCE, Greece. Cleveland Museum of Art, Gift of the John Huntington Art and Polytechnic Trust, 1927.489. 3D scan published by the Cleveland Museum of Art under CC0 1.0.
- **Publication requirement:** Credit is not legally required under CC0; retain museum/object provenance as good curatorial practice.
- **Art-context review:** Adult mythological female nude; no sexual act. Source thumbnail inspected.

Suggested original description: A small terracotta Aphrodite shifts her weight onto one leg, with her torso following a gentle curve. The museum connects this figure to the famous Aphrodite of Knidos and suggests she may once have held a mirror. The surviving fragments preserve both its intimate scale and its poised movement.

## Eve Tempted

**Jean-Baptiste Carpeaux, 1874.** Bronze. Approximately 71.44 × 24.13 × 24.13 cm (28 1/8 × 9 1/2 × 9 1/2 in.). Minneapolis Institute of Art, accession 2016.33.29.

[Object record](https://collections.artsmia.org/art/124351/eve-tempted-jean-baptiste-carpeaux) · [3D model](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/eve-tempted-c-1874-by-jean-baptiste-carpeaux-b875536cc0c741349fe632b32e4ec975) · [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/)

- **Object identity:** Museum object directly linked by museum-published scan; inscription and bronze appearance agree. Use museum date 1874 rather than scan title’s c.1874.
- **Artwork rights:** Public Domain in the museum object API.
- **Scan attribution:** 3D scan: Minneapolis Institute of Art (Mia); research led by Michael Tetzlaff, University of Wisconsin–Stout, with Cultural Heritage Imaging and Mia; model building and customization by Lou Brown. Textures generated from photographic data using Kintsugi 3D Builder. Licensed CC BY-SA 4.0.
- **Publication requirement:** Display attribution, model-source link, license link and actual modifications. Keep adapted model files under CC BY-SA 4.0. Do not imply endorsement.
- **Art-context review:** Adult biblical female nude; no sexual act. Source thumbnail inspected.

Suggested original description: Carpeaux places Eve beside the tree in the Garden of Eden, turning the biblical episode into a compact study of gesture and movement. The bronze figure brings a hand toward her mouth as a serpent coils around the support behind her.

## Les Trois Grâces (The Three Graces)

**Jean-Baptiste Carpeaux, c. 1872.** Plaster model. 82.55 × 46.99 cm (32 1/2 × 18 1/2 in.); museum supplies two dimensions. Minneapolis Institute of Art, accession 75.52a, b.

[Object record](https://collections.artsmia.org/art/2224/les-trois-graces-jean-baptiste-carpeaux) · [3D model](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/les-trois-graces-c-1872-ce-44a0231626254f76938048d902154610) · [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/)

- **Object identity:** Scan’s tinyurl.com/3wvzxfue resolves to Mia object 2224. This is the Mia plaster, not the Musée d’Orsay or Petit Palais object.
- **Artwork rights:** Public Domain in the museum object API.
- **Scan attribution:** 3D scan: Minneapolis Institute of Art (Mia); research led by Michael Tetzlaff, University of Wisconsin–Stout, with Cultural Heritage Imaging and Mia; model building and customization by Lou Brown. Licensed CC BY-SA 4.0.
- **Publication requirement:** Display attribution, model-source link, license link and actual modifications. Keep adapted model files under CC BY-SA 4.0. Do not imply endorsement.
- **Art-context review:** Adult female mythological dancers; no sexual act. Source thumbnail inspected. The principal figures are adults; do not use unrelated Petit Palais variant metadata, which mentions a putto.

Suggested original description: Three dancing figures link hands in this plaster group related to Carpeaux’s Dance for the Paris Opéra. Raised arms, bent knees and turning bodies carry the eye around the sculpture. Its lively treatment of the female figure reflects the naturalism that made the larger composition controversial.

## Bacchante

**Teodor Rygier, 1887.** Bronze. 199 × 93 × 73 cm. National Museum in Kraków, Gallery of 19th-Century Polish Art in Sukiennice, accession MNK II-rz-276.

[Object record](https://www.imnk.pl/gallerybox.php?dir=SU491&lang=EN&more=1) · [3D model](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/bacchante-a6fbf51d8c694fb99a67580ad2c138b3) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

- **Object identity:** Scan description identifies accession MNK II-rz-276 and Sukiennice collection; museum title, artist, date, material and image agree.
- **Artwork rights:** Historical artwork from 1887 by an artist who died in 1913; public-domain assessment based on these dates. The model publisher expressly grants CC BY 4.0 for the scan.
- **Scan attribution:** 3D digitisation: Regional Digitisation Lab, Małopolska Institute of Culture in Kraków, Poland, “Virtual Museums of Małopolska” Project. Physical sculpture: National Museum in Kraków, Gallery of 19th-Century Polish Art in Sukiennice, MNK II-rz-276. Licensed CC BY 4.0.
- **Publication requirement:** Display the digitization team and project, model-source link, license link and actual modifications. Do not imply endorsement.
- **Art-context review:** Adult female mythological dancer with partially draped body; no sexual act. Source thumbnail inspected.

Suggested original description: Rygier’s bronze dancer raises a goblet while holding a vine branch, identifying her with the celebrations of Dionysus. Drapery gathers at her hips as her upper body leans into the movement. The detailed sandals and animated expression bring an observed, individual quality to the mythological subject.

## Psyche

**Auguste Rodin, 1899.** Marble. 73.66 × 68.58 × 38.1 cm (29 × 27 × 15 in.). Museum of Fine Arts, Boston, accession 1975.738.

[Object record](https://www.frenchsculpture.org/index.php/Detail/objects/29564) · [3D model](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/psyche-by-auguste-rodin-0bf828410cc14c959fdf732e213742de) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

- **Object identity:** Community scan title, museum location and visible composition agree with MFA Psyche, accession 1975.738. Mesh quality still requires acquisition QA; source listing has 46,230 faces.
- **Artwork rights:** French Sculpture Census explicitly lists artist copyright as public domain; 1899 object documented at MFA and Getty CONA.
- **Scan attribution:** “Psyche by Auguste Rodin” 3D scan by avi (https://sketchfab.com/avi), captured at the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston using Polycam photo mode. Licensed CC BY 4.0.
- **Publication requirement:** Credit avi as scan creator, link the source model and CC BY 4.0, and state actual modifications. Museum object credit is separate; do not imply the scan is institutionally commissioned.
- **Art-context review:** Adult mythological female nude in a narrative moment; no sexual act. Source thumbnail inspected.

Suggested original description: Psyche leans forward beneath an open cloak, poised as if trying to see her sleeping lover. Rodin sets the softly modeled figure against sweeping folds and a roughly worked marble base. The sculpture was exhibited in Paris in 1900 before entering the collection associated with the MFA.
