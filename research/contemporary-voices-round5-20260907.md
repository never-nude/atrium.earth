## Curatorial and capture notes

This selection broadens the list of artists rather than adding multiple works by the same maker. It includes stone sculpture, public art, ceramics, a plaster maquette and an artist-published digital work. Artist prominence is not used as a public label.

- **Moth:** Official university model identifies its collection and bronze medium. The publisher spells the artist surname Thompson; artist and gallery references identify the New Zealand moth sculptor as Elizabeth Thomson.
- **Goat:** Scan contributor identifies artist, material and precise location. The foundry documents Turpin’s related practice of cast-iron public sculpture, but does not date this particular goat.
- **Compotier:** Source contributor identifies artist and series, links the artist site and supplies approximate scale. Exact creation date and the measured axis are not stated.
- **White Ball:** Primary scan metadata credits Laurence de Maistre and identifies ceramic medium. White Ball is an editorial correction of the source title While ball; it is not presented as a verified formal artist title.
- **Elephant:** Title, artist, serpentine and 57 cm height come from the scan contributor. University of Twente documents another Chisute sculpture in its collection. The contributor’s biographical death date conflicts with other references and is not reproduced.
- **Data-Confluences 006: Africa:** Artist’s primary model dates the series to 2021 and credits the Angola contributor. Artist website describes the project, created in Fortaleza, Brazil. Filed with the artist’s Brazilian practice rather than treating the title Africa as the work’s geographical origin.
- **L’homme libre:** Artist, date, bronze and Fred Ducros credit are explicit in scan metadata. Catalog title is the opening phrase of the contributor’s description; a separate formal title and dimensions are not recorded.
- **La Précarité de la Paix:** Commissioner’s 2007 publication dates the work and inauguration to 2003 and identifies patinated bronze and the address. This primary record takes precedence over 2001 in the scan title.
- **Sténos (maquette):** Primary scan explicitly describes a maquette. The museum documents plaster maquettes made from wax casts of the artist’s hands, then scanned and enlarged; the finished installation entered macLYON. Do not attribute museum ownership or full installation dimensions to this maquette.
- **Vatete (Old Auntie):** Artist, title, translation, stone and height are supplied by the primary scan contributor; local tourism authority confirms the sculpture park.
- **Shiva Nataraja (Lord of Dance):** Museum collection record and 2016 spotlight name all three makers, give the date, dimensions, bronze medium, foundry and accession. Contemporary manufacture is explicit in the museum’s documentation.

The source and 3D model credits remain attached to every work, including the three Tamil Nadu makers, Fred Ducros’s bronze contribution, and Ruy Cezar Campos’s environmental recording. Model-file licenses are recorded separately from underlying artwork rights. Six exact creation dates are unrecorded; no scan or upload date is substituted for an artwork date.
