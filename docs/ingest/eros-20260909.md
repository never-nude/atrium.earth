# Body and desire — 30 additions, 9 September 2026

Adds 30 distinct works to production commit `be2802ddf31e50285961067c2fa3d01df618eb09`, taking the public collection from 660 to 690 works (698 catalog records, including eight hidden records). The existing catalog entries and viewing settings are preserved.

The selection explores non-graphic adult nudity, lovers, bathers, divine couples, and representations of the body. It includes sculpture from prehistoric to modern times, with work connected to France, Italy, Greece, Belgium, Germany, Austria, Spain, Portugal, Malta, India, Thailand, and Papua New Guinea. Cultural figures retain their source context; uncertain dates and attributions remain qualified.

## Metadata and licenses

The source for each downloaded file is its explicitly licensed Zenodo/3DBigDataSpace archive record. Fresh Sketchfab metadata was retrieved wherever available and saved separately. Attribution includes the named scan author, source record, license, and the changes made for Atrium. The license applies to the archived version linked in the record; it does not assert a license for a different current download.

Several source pages now have a different license or are unavailable. Adonis is archived as CC0 although its former Sketchfab URL returns 404. The archived Psyche, Cupid and Psyche, and Esquiline Venus files carry CC BY 4.0; current Sketchfab metadata lists different terms. Moreau’s Venus archive also carries CC BY 4.0, and its current description expressly requests credit, although the current API has no license object. These cases are recorded in the manifest and link to the licensed archive. [Creative Commons explains that its licenses cannot be revoked while their conditions are followed](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).

Current museum records correct the scan captions where possible: the reclining Clésinger Bacchante is dated 1848 and is distinct from Woman Bitten by a Snake; Thorvaldsen’s marble Cupid and Psyche is A27, carved in 1861; the original plaster Psyche is A26, from 1806. The Louvre dates the lead Three Nymphs to 1930. The Cognacq-Jay bather is attributed to Joseph or Ignace Broche. Berlin’s reclining Pomona is terracotta by the Gormann workshop, 1845–1855. MNAT’s Pomona is marble, mid-second century, inventory 378.

The Thai Embracing Couple retains the museum’s current date and ware attribution; the unusual combination is explicitly attributed to the museum rather than silently redated. The Malta scan does not establish whether the photographed object is an original or a reproduction; the public note preserves that uncertainty. Bipi, Selhariz, and several other objects have no supported creation date. Vienna is recorded as a documented location, not a verified place of manufacture.

## Derivatives and verification

All original GLBs were verified against archive checksums and validated as self-contained glTF 2 files. The 30 optimized previews total 50.93 MB. Source textures remain intact; specular-glossiness materials were converted where necessary for the viewer.

Source node transforms were baked for the Uffizi reclining figures, with world-space bounds verified unchanged. Disconnected museum-wall geometry behind Pomona was removed while preserving the sculpture. Dark bronze exposure was adjusted for the Lambeaux group and Eve. All rotations, framing, and exposure settings are shared by the thumbnail renderer and interactive viewer.

The manifest records source and derivative checksums, triangle counts, viewing settings, thumbnail hashes, and public asset verification. Each preview is published at an immutable content-hashed URL on Atrium’s existing model domain.

Validation covers: 30 unique additions, all 668 baseline records unchanged, wing classification, local asset verification, production build, compiled viewer settings, thumbnail and poster presence, and public model hashes. The development-only review page is `/preview/body-and-desire/`; production builds exclude it.
