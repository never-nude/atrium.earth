# Animals, real and imagined — 30 additions, 9 September 2026

Adds 30 distinct works to production commit `3d6de40e225145ad2c3bbdedefe9d4014c3a7151`, taking the public collection from 750 to 780 works (788 catalog records, including eight hidden records). Existing records and viewing settings are preserved.

The selection explores animals as guardians, companions, sacred beings, and vessels. It brings together Bronze Age Chinese bronzes, Egyptian sacred animals, a Byzantine mosaic, medieval metalwork, Peruvian ceramics, Japanese netsuke, West African masks and carvings, and modern public sculpture. An Iranian ram-shaped ewer, a Maya bird whistle, an Augsburg heraldic eagle, and a Khmer elephant jar connect animal imagery with everyday and ceremonial use. Missing dates and uncertain places remain explicit.

## Documentation and attribution

The downloaded models are CC BY 4.0 or CC0 archive versions in Zenodo / 3DBigDataSpace, including a model deposited directly by the Hunt Museum. Current Sketchfab metadata was checked where available, and its license status is recorded separately. Where current terms differ or the model is unavailable, the public source link points to the licensed archive version. Credits identify the model contributor, license, and Atrium’s geometry and texture optimization.

Museum accession numbers and titles were compared with the existing collection to exclude alternative scans of the same objects. The assembled version of the double-owl wine vessel is used instead of a scan showing its lid suspended above it. A peacock lamp was excluded because extensive surrounding geometry overwhelmed the object.

Current museum documentation corrects several archive captions. Mia places its elephant-shaped lime jar in Thailand in the eleventh to twelfth century. Its mosaic of an elephant attacking a feline comes from an early Christian church; Constantine’s villa is a comparison, not the findspot. Wawel identifies the eagle vessel as Heinrich Mannlich’s Augsburg work of around 1666. Wiener Wohnen identifies the reclining bison as Hubert Fiala’s stone sculpture. The rat-on-persimmon netsuke retains China as the country recorded by Mia.

The horse and rider from Deve Hüyük, the Harvard bird-headed plaque, the Bonn baboon, and the Maya whistle have no securely recorded creation date in the consulted sources. The carved crocodile’s possible Liberian origin and probable Kessler collection number remain qualified. The Indra relief’s location is documented as Denver Art Museum in the 2017 scan, without asserting its present whereabouts.

## Rendering and verification

Archive checksums, self-contained glTF 2 structure, and optimized previews were checked. The 30 previews total 33.46 MB. Geometry and textures were compressed for browser display, with unsupported specular-glossiness materials converted where needed.

Six views of every model were inspected before selecting its default camera. The mosaic is oriented face-on, and the Nuna crocodile plank mask stands with its tail above its head, matching the source’s description of how it is worn. Front or three-quarter views show the faces of masks and reliefs; animal vessels retain their full handles, spouts and supports. The long crocodile carving receives a higher viewpoint to show its back and limbs. Thumbnails and the viewer use the same saved transformations and material settings.

Historical losses, display supports, and small patches of scanned surroundings are retained. The Indra scan records only the carved face of its relief: the preview renders that surface from both sides so it remains visible when turned, without inventing a reverse or implying a complete scan of the stone block.

The manifest records source and preview checksums, file sizes, triangle counts, license versions, thumbnail hashes, orientation decisions, and remote model verification. Optimized models use immutable content-hashed URLs on Atrium’s existing model domain.

Release checks cover 30 unique additions, all 758 baseline records unchanged, all prior viewing settings unchanged, wing classification, asset completeness, a production build, compiled viewing settings, thumbnails and posters, and remote model hashes. The local review page is `/preview/animals-real-and-imagined/`; production builds exclude it.
