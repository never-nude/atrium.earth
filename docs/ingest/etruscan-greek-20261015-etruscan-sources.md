# Etruscan discovery — 15 October 2026

Twenty distinct physical objects or surviving archaeological elements are proposed
in [the candidate manifest](etruscan-greek-20261015-etruscan-candidates.json): six
CC0, thirteen CC BY 4.0 and one CC BY-SA 4.0. Public Sketchfab detail responses were
checked live for download availability, exact license, processing state and source
identity. None of the twenty UIDs occurs in the 1,951-record catalog. The independent
preparation audit also found no existing object-record, title or accession collision.

This is discovery, not acquisition or publication. Small publisher thumbnails were
visually reviewed for all twenty. They show the surviving objects rather than
room-dominated scenes. That screening does not certify reverse surfaces, mesh
integrity, material fidelity, scale or final presentation. Archaeological fragments
and elements remain labeled as such; a complete ancient monument, basin or roof
must not be inferred from them.

The current public v3 detail API exposes no selected-download archive map. Its face
and texture counts are identified as API-detail counts, not measured GLB geometry.
Selected GLB bytes and archive faces are unresolved in this discovery manifest and
require the authenticated metadata-only preflight before acquisition. No model
payloads, signed download URLs or credentials were obtained here.

## Exclusions and duplicate screening

- **Modern object mislabeled as ancient:** Mia UID
  `373a2d5d08af4e2292b50fe65db5478a` is titled “Etruscan Figure of a Youth,
  470–460 BCE” on Sketchfab. The [current museum API](https://search.artsmia.org/id/764)
  identifies accession 47.39 as “Figure of a Youth, in the style of an Etruscan
  bronze (5th century BC),” dated **19th century**. Held outside this ancient batch.
- **Authenticity uncertain:** Cleveland 1977.42, UID
  `fa614b0b24444d0b9b324048359ca180`, is described by the [museum](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/1977.42)
  as “Etruscan or modern forgery,” dated 700–500 BCE or the 1900s. Not counted as
  securely ancient.
- **Conflicting date/culture:** Hunt HCA 679, UID
  `b5e91e6bb07c4395b4e1981cfa8451ea`, is called Etruscan by its publisher and
  [museum description](https://www.huntmuseum.com/explore/item/700e47e2-a414-39c0-b722-f4028c5931ed/),
  but the current formal object record dates it to the **2nd century CE**. Held
  rather than silently substituting a generic Etruscan date.
- **No usable live license/download:** USF/Tampa “Ceramic Mixing Vessel on Stand,”
  UID `31cf317717a14958a708b08336dd1253`, has a detailed [institutional record](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/etruscan/9/).
  Live public model metadata returned `downloadType: "no"` and `license: null`.
  Existing processed archives do not grant download permission or a CC license.
- **Existing catalog works:** sarcophagus from Grotta Dipinta
  `b88757d7925443a4a75218c7ea4b0c45`; seated-man cinerary urn
  `094dfeedee31485ca972a99bc46b0d31`; and Roman Mithras statue
  `0d7d7e35ec3a4d318b72efa40fd31c64` are already represented. The latter is Roman,
  despite its Tarquinia holding location.
- **Duplicate reposts/compositions:** krater
  `4e99abae8a4b430cb3d496ecf429751b` duplicates original ICS
  `08fed18a76df4b88a0967592b64a322c`; MAAM oinochoe
  `e7012272814f4ada8e6b3163c2001193` duplicates the selected 231516 composition;
  two deer-head stoppers `c99a0281553e4753b8098a3ac6b0dfa8` and
  `0b77fa9868f94aa4a5ac4c49fba8de15` share their description and 238-image capture.
  TheViMu’s lyre-player/banqueter reposts do not add new objects to the museum
  originals.
- **Explicit modern reproductions:** JTorrejon jar
  `d7918fab9b774f10b9fbeffdfaf27616` is a modern potter’s replica; portunes kylix
  `2d853b1802ff49a6a131c70056255edd` is explicitly a modern reproduction. Digital
  reconstructions and altered “pure gold” versions are also excluded.
- **License exclusions:** numerous attractive GDH, Geoffrey Marchal and other
  Etruscan models carry NC or ND terms. They remain excluded without exception.
  Eligibility is checked per model; an institution’s other CC models do not change
  the terms of an NC model.

The signed Aristonothos krater, UID `c82c166302da465d952e4dcdf9e8d478`, was passed
to Greek-pottery discovery as a separate strong lead. The scanner’s opening “Roman”
label is inconsistent with its 7th-century BCE date and quoted Caere/Greek-potter
history; a Capitoline primary identification is required. It is not counted among
