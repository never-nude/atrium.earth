# Daily life and ceremony — 30 additions, 9 September 2026

Adds 30 distinct works to production commit `c9c8a6ade294a682eb4be33f674288a99e74655c`, taking the public collection from 780 to 810 works (818 catalog records, including eight hidden records). Existing records and viewing settings are preserved.

This selection explores objects shaped by use: musical instruments, writing tools, ritual vessels, household containers, and architectural woodwork. Chinese bronzes and a calligraphy brush appear alongside Cuban rattles, stone wind instruments from Chile, a Peruvian whistling jar, Iranian and Iraqi ceramics, Vietnamese celadon, a Fang offering box, Polish liturgical metalwork, and a Roman lamp stand from Cambrils. A carved Mamluk door panel and a printed bath-tablet box connect large architectural surfaces with objects held in the hand.

## Documentation and attribution

The models are CC BY 4.0 or CC0 versions documented in Zenodo / 3DBigDataSpace. Current Sketchfab records were also checked. Some archive search and download requests timed out; direct file endpoints and the original public Objaverse mirror supplied the files. Every downloaded model, including mirror copies, matched the archive’s published size and checksum. Retrieval URLs are recorded separately from the archival source URLs. Credits identify the contributor, license and Atrium’s geometry and texture optimization.

Museum accession numbers, model identifiers and object titles were compared with the existing collection to exclude duplicate scans. The two Cuban rattles are one cataloged pair. Only the body model of Cleveland’s snuff bottle is included; its stopper is not counted as a second work.

Current collection records resolve several caption discrepancies. The Fitzwilliam dates its cloisonné incense burner to the eighteenth century, earlier than the scan caption, and identifies it as part of an altar set. Världskulturmuseerna records the Cuban rattles as gourds, with lengths of 23 and 24 centimeters and accession 1979.15.0005a-b. Cleveland dates its snuff bottle to 1736–1795 or later. Mia’s current catalog supplies the dates and titles of its bowls, ewers, bronze vessels, door panel and epitaph. The Catalan heritage authority identifies the Roman lamp stand with the Museu d’Història de Cambrils and the villa of La Llosa.

Collecting dates are not substituted for creation dates. The Chilean instruments’ nineteenth-century donations and publication history, the rattles’ acquisition in 1979, and Andersson’s collecting in China do not establish when those objects were made. The Stockholm bottle’s date remains “probably 1764.” The Gothic chalice’s possible workshop locations remain qualified. No precise date is invented for the Fang offering box or the Maya incense vessel.

## Rendering and verification

All 30 source files passed checksum and self-contained glTF 2 checks. Optimized previews total 35.78 MB. Geometry and textures were compressed for browser use, with unsupported specular-glossiness materials converted where needed.

Six views of each work were reviewed. Elevated camera positions reveal the interiors of painted bowls and the decorated faces of the drum and bath-tablet box while preserving the objects’ level orientation. Vessels retain handles, spouts, lids and feet. Figures and carved panels open toward the viewer. The saved camera, model rotation, framing and material settings also generate the thumbnail.

Original damage, detached elements, display supports and untextured areas of the source scans remain visible. The woman-shaped bottle is a historical fragment with its head-stopper missing. The Xu vessel has incomplete texture coverage on its reverse, and the Yong bell retains a pale untextured area at its lower opening. The door panel and epitaph are surface scans: their carved face can appear mirrored from behind, rather than showing a separately captured reverse. A scan records the visible surfaces of an object and does not establish that hidden interiors were captured.

The manifest records source and preview checksums, file sizes, triangle counts, licenses, retrieval URLs, thumbnail hashes, orientation decisions and remote asset verification. Models are served from immutable content-hashed URLs on Atrium’s existing model domain.

Release checks cover 30 unique additions, all 788 baseline records unchanged, all prior viewing settings unchanged, wing assignment, asset completeness, a production build, compiled viewing settings, thumbnails and posters, and remote model hashes. The local review page is `/preview/daily-life-and-ceremony/`; production builds exclude it.
