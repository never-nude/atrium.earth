# Forms of desire — 30 additions, 9 September 2026

Adds 30 distinct works to production commit `aa69ec1b43d5f0bdbd5172748a21641ac8b19c82`, taking the public collection from 720 to 750 works (758 catalog records, including eight hidden records). Existing records and viewing settings are preserved.

The selection explores erotic art through adult nudes, lovers embracing, reclining figures, and interpretations of Venus. Ancient Roman fragments sit alongside Baroque nymphs, Art Nouveau bronzes, modern sculpture, and artist-published figures. Documented locations and traditions include India, Finland, Austria, Belgium, Czechia, France, Germany, Poland, Russia, Slovakia, Spain, the United Kingdom, and the United States. Several contributors provide no secure place or date; those gaps remain explicit.

## Documentation and attribution

The downloaded models are CC BY 4.0 or CC0 archive versions in Zenodo / 3DBigDataSpace. Current Sketchfab metadata was checked where available, and its license status is recorded separately. Where current terms differ, the public source link points to the licensed archive version. Credits identify the model contributor, license, and Atrium’s geometry and texture optimization.

Museum and municipal documentation resolves several ambiguous scan captions. NCMA now calls its Roman fragment Torso of a Statue Wearing a Hip Mantle, leaving its earlier identification as an emperor in the guise of Jupiter open. Jim Dine’s bronze belongs to Looking Toward the Avenue. Wiener Wohnen identifies the sculptor of Music-Making Faun as Hannes Haslecker and dates it to 1958–1962. Louvre documentation establishes the dates and dimensions of Nymph Arethusa.

The Brassempouy Torso is explicitly labeled as Roselyne Conil’s 2002 enlarged reproduction. No prehistoric date is assigned to Glen Cram’s Venus of Teniszfélhold: its source gives no archaeological findspot or dating. The particular cast or version of Barrias’s Nature Unveiling Herself remains unspecified. Parvati retains the source’s devotional identification. Missing artist, date, material, and location information is not invented.

## Rendering and verification

Archive checksums, self-contained glTF 2 structure, and optimized previews were checked. The 30 previews total 40.75 MB. Source geometry and textures were compressed for browser display, with unsupported specular-glossiness materials converted for the viewer.

Six views of every model were inspected before choosing the default camera. Sculptures remain upright and fully framed; front and three-quarter views show the figures, gestures, and embrace. The default view and generated thumbnail use identical saved camera, rotation, framing, and material settings. Historical fragments remain fragments; supports and small patches of scanned surroundings are retained. A Bern candidate was excluded because its sculpture was overwhelmed by surrounding fountain geometry.

The manifest records source and preview checksums, file sizes, triangle counts, license versions, thumbnail hashes, orientation decisions, and remote model verification. Optimized models use immutable content-hashed URLs on Atrium’s existing model domain.

Release checks cover 30 unique additions, all 728 baseline records unchanged, all prior viewing settings unchanged, wing classification, asset completeness, a production build, compiled viewing settings, thumbnails and posters, and remote model hashes. The local review page is `/preview/forms-of-desire/`; production builds exclude it.
