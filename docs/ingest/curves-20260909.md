# The other side — 30 additions, 9 September 2026

Adds 30 distinct works to production commit `3dc6f2a401c6118e06550e16a8e9ffe635fe4f19`, taking the public collection from 690 to 720 works (728 catalog records, including eight hidden records). Existing records and viewing settings are preserved.

The selection centers on the buttocks and rear contours of adult sculptural figures: crouching Aphrodites, athletes, bathers, torsos, mythological figures, and an explicitly labeled artist-created digital study. The sources are predominantly European and Roman; the batch also includes work associated with North Africa and the United States. Geographic and chronological breadth remains bounded by the availability of documented, openly licensed models with usable rear surfaces.

## Documentation and attribution

Downloaded files are licensed Zenodo / 3DBigDataSpace archive versions. Current Sketchfab metadata was also inspected and its license status is recorded separately. Where present-day terms differ, the public source link identifies the explicitly licensed archive. Credits name the source author and license, and identify Atrium’s geometry and texture optimization. The archive license is not presented as permission for a different current download.

Museum documentation takes precedence over contradictory scan captions. NCMA’s Hercules is now identified as an artificially weathered, late-eighteenth-century work made in Rome, rather than a Roman antiquity. Middelheim describes Assia as an idealized figure inspired by Assia Granatouroff. Glyptotek scholarship identifies its Three Graces as a nineteenth-century workshop copy after Canova. The Hunt Museum specifies seventeenth-century polychromed limewood for Apollo. Allard Pierson identifies the silver inlays and Italian manufacture of Mercurius Arvernus.

Dates, materials, and locations absent from reliable source documentation remain unspecified. Danaë’s scan does not establish which cast was photographed. The Trypillian attribution follows the scan author; no exact findspot, date, collection, or original-versus-reproduction status is established. The Punishment is credited as Deepak CC’s digital interpretation of the Farnese Hercules, not as a museum scan.

## Rendering and verification

Original files were checked against archive checksums and validated as self-contained glTF 2 models. The 30 optimized previews total 40.96 MB. Materials using specular-glossiness were converted for the existing viewer. Texture and geometry compression preserves the source sculpture’s appearance.

Each starting view was inspected visually. Rear and three-quarter cameras emphasize the selected body contours while keeping complete sculptures upright and framed. Several candidates were rejected because their rear scans were open, incomplete, or obscured by drapery or background geometry. Thumbnail and interactive viewer share the saved rotation, camera direction, framing, and appearance settings.

The machine-readable manifest records source and preview checksums, sizes, triangle counts, licensed source versions, thumbnail hashes, and public model verification. Previews use immutable content-hashed URLs on Atrium’s existing model domain.

Validation covers 30 unique additions, all 698 baseline records unchanged, wing classification, asset completeness, a production build, compiled viewing settings, thumbnail and poster presence, and remote model hashes. The development-only review page is `/preview/rear-views/`; production builds exclude it.
